# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Interior designers, architects, and affluent homeowners in Mexico furnishing high-end residential projects. They arrive skeptical of "marble furniture" as a category and need to see craftsmanship and taste before they'll start a WhatsApp quote conversation. The site speaks Spanish (es-MX) and serves both the professional specifier (materials, dimensions, customization) and the private client (emotion, trust).

## Product Purpose

A brand showcase for K+A Mármoles' marble furniture collection. Success is a visitor browsing the collection, opening a piece, and starting a WhatsApp quote conversation. Secondary: the site makes K+A look like a peer of the international marble ateliers (Salvatori, Antolini), justifying premium made-to-order pricing. The design itself is the sales argument — collection pages are a showcase, not a store.

## Positioning

Every piece is cut from a single unrepeatable slab and fabricated to measure in Mexico — atelier-level marble furniture without importing from Italy.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: **Cotizar por WhatsApp** (immediate, conversational quoting). Secondary fallback: written inquiry via the Contacto page (form / email) for visitors not ready to chat.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "Muebles de mármol a la medida, hechos en México a nivel de atelier europeo."
- Belief ladder: (1) marble furniture can look refined, not kitsch — the photography and restraint prove the category; (2) K+A specifically has taste and craft — the collection and named marble varieties prove the maker; (3) made-to-order in Mexico is credible and reachable — dimensions, fabrication notes, and an immediate WhatsApp channel prove the process. The site's job is beliefs 1 and 2; WhatsApp handles 3.
- Proof on hand: real product photography of the 10 pieces (currently low-res placeholders in `public/productos/`, hi-res coming from the client under the same filenames), named marble varieties per piece, made-to-measure fabrication in Mexico, and the K+A Design parent studio. No press, awards, testimonials, or client logos exist yet — never fabricate any; credibility comes from the material and the presentation itself.

## Brand Personality

Sober, precise, monumental. Quiet confidence — the stone speaks and the design stays out of its way. Museum-like restraint over gallery coolness or rustic-artisanal warmth. The voice is assured and unhurried, in Spanish, and treats the visitor as someone with taste.

## Anti-references

- Stone distributor / countertop contractor sites (Marble.com-style slab grids, per-m² pricing, promos).
- Generic furniture e-commerce: carts, prices, discount badges, cluttered transactional grids.
- Rustic-artisanal styling.
- Template SaaS looks, bright white backgrounds, rounded cards with drop shadows.
- The bar: it should sit next to salvatoriofficial.com and antolini.com without embarrassment.

## Design Principles

1. **The stone is the protagonist.** Photography and material textures carry the page; UI chrome recedes. Never crowd a piece.
2. **Restraint proves the category.** Belief 1 (marble furniture ≠ kitsch) is won through sobriety — generous space, few elements, no ornament the stone didn't earn.
3. **Precision signals fabrication.** Hairline rules, exact alignment, and stated dimensions echo millimetric stonework; sloppiness anywhere undermines the craft claim.
4. **Every path ends at a conversation.** Each surface should make starting a WhatsApp quote feel natural and low-commitment, with a written-inquiry fallback always reachable.
5. **Never fabricate proof.** No invented testimonials, press, or logos — show real pieces, real marbles, real process.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Keyboard navigable with visible focus states (brass outline convention already in place). `prefers-reduced-motion` respected — already implemented, keep it. Sufficient contrast for gray-on-black secondary text (`--veta` on `--negro` needs watching). Alt text on all product images. Spanish-language semantics (`lang="es"`).
